# limitations_student_models_high_demand_contexts
Limitations of the Student Model in High-Demand Contexts: A Structured Review of Underrepresented Functional State Variables

Adaptive Learning Systems (ALS) and Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) personalize instruction from a student's knowledge state, yet their student models are built exclusively from data the system itself generates. Functional State Variables (FSV) — sleep quality, mental fatigue, perceived stress, accumulated physical load — originate outside the system, vary daily, and modulate the cognitive availability a student brings to each session. The omission matters little under stable conditions, but becomes critical when functional state fluctuates intensely and predictably. Students combining higher education with elite sport are the clearest case: training load, competition phase and academic pressure are routinely quantified there and affect both performances. This structured review analyzes 70 ALS and ITS with an identifiable student model, published 1994–2024 and purposively sampled to span the field's main modelling paradigms, venues and temporal range; reporting follows PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-ScR. FSV are almost entirely absent, even though the compilation was drawn from systems explicitly addressing adaptation to the student, where they were most likely to appear. No system models sleep quality, physical training load, perceived stress or competition phase. Mental fatigue and the related cognitive load appear in three systems only (4.3%), and in all three the estimate is decoupled from instructional decisions: the state is detected but never acts. Lexical analysis of the full 3,926-record verification compilation reproduces this pattern. We propose a taxonomy of six FSV categories and three open research questions setting an agenda for functional-state-aware student models.
